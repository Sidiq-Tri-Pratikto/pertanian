# Reusable sentiment for national resilience

**Update, 31 October 2026 WIB:** the subsequent TNI/Kartika-focused search adds documented field use of sentiment analysis, recruiting assistance and service feedback. See [Operational app precedents for Kartika](Operational%20app%20precedents%20for%20Kartika.md) and its [source ledger](../research_notes/Operational%20app%20precedents%20for%20Kartika/sources.md). NarrativeLens remains the first choice, now with a disaster-communication demonstration; REKRUT AMAN is the document-based fallback. The cross-sector selection criteria below record the earlier 26 October scope. Dated additions separate actual adoption from our proposed capabilities.

**Recommend NarrativeLens: a reusable dashboard that compares how different sources discuss the same event, with target-specific sentiment and evidence behind every finding.** The 26 October recommendation starts with a service disruption and demonstrates reuse on public emergency information. Its selection criterion is cross-sector use, while the 31 October follow-up prioritises the TNI/Kartika application. Foreign evidence now includes field use as well as exercises, research and benchmarks; these maturity levels remain distinct. Indonesia already has commercial sentiment, narrative-monitoring and response tools. **The defensible opportunity is a narrow, measurable workflow improvement; public research cannot establish that an equivalent is absent from Indonesia's private or defence systems.** The five directions below are alternatives, not five modules to build. The baseline uses public material reviewed through **26 October 2026, WIB**, with additions marked **31 October**; proposed architectures and benefits remain untested.

## A generic product can retain a concrete defence application

Your preference changes the selection criterion: choose a reusable user task before choosing an institution. Comparing incident coverage, investigating rating–comment inconsistencies, and evaluating language errors are distinct tasks that can survive beyond one competition. A TNI-specific taxonomy, intake process or programme name would reduce that flexibility without necessarily improving the core product.

The competition still matters. The official website places Sentiment Analysis Dashboard under **Siber Sosial / Cognitive Domain**, and allocates **25% to relevance, 25% innovation, 25% prototype functionality, 15% implementation feasibility and 10% documentation**. The 31 October check clarifies that relevance explicitly concerns TNI AD IT/OT security needs. A service or humanitarian scenario must explain that connection; a military user alone does not establish category fit. The submission deadline is **15 November 2026**. Detailed rules governing pre-existing work, subsequent reuse and intellectual property were not verified from the publicly accessible material, so confirm those before assuming unrestricted reuse of the submitted work. ([Official competition website](https://kartikacyberhackathon.id/))

The recommended positioning is: **“An evidence-backed tool for understanding how the same issue is perceived across sources; public-service resilience is one application.”** Examples include a logistics outage, product recall, campus announcement, evacuation update or public rescue operation. The common decision is which concern or interpretation an analyst should inspect. The product should analyse documents and aggregate issues; it has no need to score individual loyalty, political beliefs or hostile intent.

Keep the analytical vocabulary precise. Sentiment describes evaluation of a target or aspect; stance describes a position toward a specified proposition. Expressed emotion, urgency and factual support are separate. “The rescue team helped, but the shelter instructions were confusing” contains different evaluations of two aspects. A whole-document negative score discards that distinction; neither score establishes a security threat.

## Foreign defence precedents validate sensemaking, with different maturity levels

**The 31 October update adds field use during Hurricane Helene in 2024.** The XVIII Airborne Corps public affairs office visualised Sprinklr sentiment in Maven, according to an Army report published on 22 December 2025. This is distinct from SETAF-AF's experimentation in the same article. It establishes incident use, without classifier accuracy or a controlled time-saving benchmark. ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/289747/setaf_af_public_affairs_experiment_with_emerging_technology_to_shape_the_information_environment))

**The UK Dstl's work during NATO Exercise Trident Juncture remains an exercise-use precedent, reported in January 2019.** For five weeks, analysts examined hashtags, sentiment, external media and NATO communications, informing adjustments to exercise communications. This documents use during a training exercise. It does not establish a permanently deployed dashboard, autonomous decision-making or independently measured communication impact. Its reusable lesson is to connect observed reactions to a specific communication task. ([UK Ministry of Defence/Dstl](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/dstl-tests-natos-war-of-words))

**US DARPA INCAS was a research programme** addressing detection and tracking of influence campaigns, including quantified confidence and connections across messaging. Teams were announced in 2021; the programme page now marks it complete. Those facts establish funded research and stated objectives, not operational performance. Borrow evidence linkage and uncertainty; a hackathon sentiment product need not reproduce campaign attribution or psychographic analysis. ([DARPA announcement](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2021/incas-program), [programme status and aims](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/influence-campaign-awareness-and-sensemaking))

**Canada's DND/CAF IDEaS results list The Dark Crawler as funded R&D**, explicitly combining textual analysis, sentiment analysis and machine learning. The published award is CAD1,005,295.50. This is a competitive development award, not a hackathon victory or proof of successful armed-forces deployment. It establishes defence interest in the capability; it supplies no verified algorithm or accuracy benchmark for us to reuse. ([Canadian Department of National Defence](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/programs/defence-ideas/element/competitive-projects/challenges/detecting-responding-hostile-information-activities.html))

**Australia's INSIGHT is an adjacent information-analysis precedent.** A September 2026 Defence article describes a university/DSTG platform bringing together heterogeneous sources, connections and updated briefings on premises. It does not explicitly verify a sentiment classifier or operational adoption. Separately, Melbourne's 2023 RAPID case study describes topic/network analysis and event comparisons tested with defence researchers; this is partner-reported research and trials. Their useful inspiration is a reusable evidence-to-briefing workflow. ([Australian Defence: INSIGHT](https://www.defence.gov.au/news-events/news/2026-09-16/platform-deals-blow-disinformation), [Melbourne: RAPID](https://research.unimelb.edu.au/partnerships/case-studies/ai-platform-targets-crowd-sourced-data-for-military-intelligence))

**NATO research supplies a reason to emphasise reliability.** Its Strategic Communications Centre of Excellence published a 2026 evaluation of stance/sentiment across English, Lithuanian and Russian, reporting persistent language gaps. This is benchmark research, not evidence about Indonesian performance. The 2020 Public Affairs Handbook also warns that routine military vocabulary can distort sentiment classification and calls for human involvement. Together they justify domain testing rather than confidence in an off-the-shelf classifier. ([NATO StratCom COE study](https://stratcomcoe.org/publications/understanding-llm-performance-gaps-strategic-implications-of-stance-detection-and-sentiment-analysis-in-small-languages/341), [NATO handbook, printed p.152](https://www.act.nato.int/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/nato-pao-handbook-2020.pdf))

## Indonesia already has the core capabilities; narrower gaps remain hypotheses

Drone Emprit markets media monitoring, trends and sentiment. NoLimit offers sentiment dashboards, emerging topics and comparisons; its Care product includes case management, handling metrics and SLA notifications. Kazee advertises sentiment/emotion, clustering, crisis alerts and AI assistance. These vendor pages establish **available offerings**, not independently audited accuracy. Generic topics, alerts, summaries and a defence use case therefore provide a weak novelty claim. ([Drone Emprit](https://mediakernels.com/our-products/drone-emprit/), [NoLimit Dashboard](https://nolimit.id/nolimit-dashboard), [NoLimit Care](https://nolimit.id/nolimit-care), [Kazee](https://kazee.id/media-monitoring-jkt))

There is institutional evidence too: a November 2025 Ditjenpas report describes Cipinang prison developing a digital early-warning pilot with Indonesia Indicator for sentiment and issue escalation. eBdesk currently markets event intelligence and a national-security AI framework. Consequently, **“defence/crisis sentiment intelligence has not been implemented in Indonesia” is unsupported**. Public sources leave narrower questions unanswered: source/time-matched comparisons, traceable target-level evidence, measured abstention, and reproducible domain-error reporting. Those are documentation gaps, not proof that vendors lack the features. ([Ditjenpas](https://www.ditjenpas.go.id/lapas-cipinang-kembangkan-sistem-pemantauan-digital-untuk-antisipasi-krisis-komunikasi-pemasyarakatan), [eBdesk products](https://www.ebdesk.com/product.html))

Indonesia presents credible testing contexts. Ombudsman's 2025 reporting documents a substantial service-feedback stream; aggregate reporting is not an accessible text corpus or proof of unresolved cases. NusaX provides parallel sentiment data for Indonesian, English and ten local languages, supporting language evaluation while leaving organic code-switching and domain vocabulary to separate tests. ([Ombudsman press release](https://ombudsman.go.id/news/download/ombudsman-ri-terima-23596-aduan-sepanjang-2025-tegaskan-warisan-pengawasan-berdampak-di-akhir-masa-jabatan-2021-2026), [NusaX paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.eacl-main.57/))

Earlier winners offer bounded inspiration. **Bupa won silver** in Databricks' 2025 APJ Smart Business Insights Challenge for an engine flagging emotional mismatches in feedback. **Echodex won expert.ai's brand/company/product sentiment category in 2021**, combining sources; **Multilingual sentiment analyser received third prize**, combining translation, review analysis and search. These awards verify recognition, not production accuracy or why the projects won. They support focused comparison and review tasks rather than a promise that the same feature set will win Kartika. ([Databricks organiser](https://hackathon.stackup.dev/web/events/databricks-smart-business-insights-challenge), [expert.ai organiser gallery](https://expertai-nlapi-042021.devpost.com/project-gallery), [Echodex](https://devpost.com/software/echodex), [Multilingual sentiment analyser](https://devpost.com/software/multilingual-review-analyser))

## Five alternatives solve different reusable jobs

All five names and designs below are proposals. Their Indonesian pain points are hypotheses unless a specific source establishes the narrower fact. Each needs a data partner or a legally reusable corpus; none is established as first in Indonesia.

### 1. NarrativeLens compares perceptions of the same event

**User job and Indonesian problem.** Communication and research teams need to distinguish criticism of a service from sadness about an incident or praise for responders. The hypothesis is that aggregate scores obscure these targets and mix changing source composition. Existing Indonesian monitoring establishes a relevant market, not the frequency or cost of this error.

**Technical solution and MVP.** Import an authorised CSV or permitted article corpus with source, time and original text. Deduplicate syndicated material, let a reviewer confirm one event and its targets, then extract aspect–sentiment pairs with evidence spans. Compare source groups within the same time window and aspect; show denominators, exclusions and uncertainty. Add stance only for an explicit proposition with defensible labels. Start with one event, four aspects and roughly 150–300 documents as a proposed demo scope. An analyst opens the quotations behind a divergence and exports a short cited brief. Counts describe the observed corpus, not national opinion.

**Reuse and optional defence.** The same workflow serves product incidents, recall coverage, campus announcements and infrastructure disruptions. A second template can compare emergency instructions with observed questions and reactions. The Helene field case and UK/NATO exercise support the communication-assessment task; RAPID provides adjacent inspiration for event comparison. None verifies our proposed event matching or target-level classifier.

**Existing alternatives and bounded gap.** NoLimit, Drone Emprit, Kazee and eBdesk already cover monitoring/intelligence. The proposed wedge is transparent matching, correct sentiment targets and auditable evidence, whose exact combination was not established in reviewed pages. ([NoLimit Dashboard](https://nolimit.id/nolimit-dashboard), [eBdesk](https://www.ebdesk.com/product.html))

**Evaluation and hardest assumption.** Evaluate both methods against the same target-level gold labels: assign the whole-document baseline's polarity to each target, then compare macro-F1 with target-aware predictions. Also measure event matching precision, citation support and analyst task time. Hold out entire events and later periods. The hardest assumption is that event/target matching is reliable enough that apparent disagreement reflects documents rather than selection mistakes. This is the recommended direction because it has a clear analyst task and a manageable corpus-based demo.

### 2. ResponseCheck verifies whether an issue recurs after a response

**User job and Indonesian problem.** Service teams need to know whether a concern returns after a case is marked resolved. Ombudsman's reporting establishes feedback volume, while NoLimit Care establishes existing workflow tools. Neither proves how often Indonesian administrative closure differs from user-confirmed resolution. That narrower problem needs partner validation.

**Technical solution and MVP.** Import de-identified case exports containing case/service identifiers, timestamped messages, response events and follow-up. Extract aspects, requested remedies and evidence of continuing failure; link recurrence using confirmed service/event identifiers and reviewable text similarity. Keep administrative closure distinct from user-confirmed outcome. The dashboard shows initial concern, response and subsequent evidence. MVP: one service incident, four aspects and a replay containing correctly resolved, recurring and ambiguous cases. Integrate an export rather than rebuilding a helpdesk.

**Reuse and optional defence.** Users include SaaS support, logistics, utilities, municipalities and university administration. A public emergency-support team could inspect whether a shelter-information response addressed recurring confusion. Foreign communication-analysis examples establish adjacent relevance; **no inspected foreign source verifies this exact defence outcome-tracking workflow**.

**Adoption update, 31 October.** The DoD's Interactive Customer Evaluation (ICE) is a documented military-service feedback precedent. Its use at Joint Base San Antonio supports collecting comments and reviewing service satisfaction, without establishing automated recurrence detection or user-confirmed resolution. Those ResponseCheck features remain proposals. ([JBSA, 30 January 2025](https://www.jbsa.mil/News/News/Article/4048110/jbsa-welcomes-new-ice-director/))

**Existing alternatives and bounded gap.** NoLimit Care already offers cases, labels and response/SLA metrics; Medallia describes sentiment and closure workflows. Assignment and “closed” buttons are therefore insufficient differentiation. The proposed gap is reproducible aspect-level recurrence and evidence of confirmed outcomes; its local availability remains unverified. ([NoLimit Care](https://nolimit.id/nolimit-care), [Medallia brochure](https://www.medallia.com/wp-content/uploads/pdf/resources/Medallia-Brochure-Social.pdf))

**Evaluation and hardest assumption.** Measure correctly linked recurrences, agreement on unresolved status and review time against a chronological queue. Keep whole incidents, ticket chains and duplicates in one split. Before/after sentiment is descriptive, not evidence that the response caused improvement. The hardest assumption is access to longitudinal follow-up with usable identifiers; without it, the product loses its distinctive job.

### 3. ScoreGap finds serious complaints hidden behind good ratings

**User job and Indonesian problem.** Survey teams need to inspect comments whose content conflicts with the associated score. The hypothesis is that rating averages hide actionable complaints in Indonesian feedback. Bupa's award supplies a foreign competition precedent, not evidence of local prevalence or the exact rating–text algorithm.

**Technical solution and MVP.** Import the original survey question, scale, rating, comment and timestamp. Extract aspect-specific sentiment and flag conservative inconsistencies, such as a high satisfaction score accompanied by an explicit unresolved service failure. Preserve question context: a high difficulty score has a different interpretation from high satisfaction. Use thresholds defined per survey scale and reviewer-checked evidence, rather than subtracting stars from an uncalibrated model probability. MVP: one five-point satisfaction survey, approximately 200–500 consented rows and an inbox comparing rating-based versus mismatch-based review. Proposed sample sizes are not statistical guarantees.

**Reuse and optional defence.** Hospitality, education, logistics, SaaS and administrative-service surveys can reuse the product. A public defence-service or relief-facility survey is an optional deployment. **No reviewed defence source establishes this precise use abroad**; its strongest precedent is civilian Bupa, and the defence application is our proposal.

**Existing alternatives and bounded gap.** Qualtrics already documents overall/topic sentiment, mixed responses and manual correction; Medallia offers related feedback workflows. Local vendors already analyse sentiment. The opportunity to test is a lightweight Indonesian-language mismatch-review workflow, not invention of aspect sentiment. ([Qualtrics documentation](https://www.qualtrics.com/support/survey-platform/data-and-analysis-module/text-iq/sentiment-analysis/))

**Evaluation and hardest assumption.** Measure reviewer-confirmed actionable inconsistencies among the first 10–20 results, false alerts and time per useful finding. Split by case/question and time; exclude labelled synthetic examples from performance claims. The hardest assumption is obtaining real rating–text pairs and agreement that flagged contradictions warrant action. Respondents need not be dishonest for their answers to contain mixed experiences.

### 4. ClarityLoop locates questions an announcement leaves unanswered

**User job and Indonesian problem.** Communication teams need to identify recurring questions that current announcements or FAQs do not adequately answer. Institutional demand for crisis communication is documented, but the prevalence and operational cost of this specific gap remain hypotheses. A question alone does not prove its author misunderstood an announcement.

**Technical solution and MVP.** Import dated approved documents and de-identified enquiries. Detect questions and explicit confusion statements, group duplicates and retrieve candidate answer passages with BM25. A reviewer confirms answered, partial or not found against the available document version. Add aspect sentiment to show which information gaps accompany negative feedback; do not infer missing answers from polarity. An optional language model drafts a clarification only from cited passages. MVP: two versions of one announcement and a replay of roughly 100–200 enquiries, showing both answered and unresolved clusters. Human approval precedes publication.

**Reuse and optional defence.** Applications include product releases, onboarding, campus notices, utility updates and public-service deadlines. Evacuation guidance or public exercise notices provide defence-relevant demonstrations. Dstl's exercise communication analysis is a direct precedent for assessing reactions, while Singapore GovTech's narrative/stance tool is a civilian implementation with reviewer editing and anonymisation. Neither verifies automatic answer-gap detection. ([GovTech AI Practice account](https://blog.ai.gov.sg/fine-tuning-language-models-for-long-context-data-automated-stance-analysis-of-citizen-discussions/))

**Existing alternatives and bounded gap.** Local monitoring, support workflows and FAQ/chatbot products create substantial overlap. The narrower proposed wedge is unanswered-question evidence linked to document versions, rather than another conversational answer interface. Availability in Indonesian competitors was not established.

**Evaluation and hardest assumption.** Measure known-answer retrieval recall, reviewer precision for missing/partial flags, false reassurance and briefing time. Hold out document versions and question threads. The hardest assumption is a complete, current document library; absent source material otherwise looks like a communication failure. Pre/post question counts alone cannot establish causal improvement.

### 5. SentimentBench exposes language and domain failures

**User job and Indonesian problem.** NLP teams need to know which classifications deserve review before using aggregate metrics. NusaX establishes a multilingual testing resource; it does not show that deployed Indonesian systems fail at a particular rate. The product hypothesis is that a reproducible local-language/domain review workflow saves more effort than scattered evaluation scripts.

**Technical solution and MVP.** Import predictions and independently human-labelled examples; compare a majority baseline, TF-IDF/logistic regression and an Indonesian encoder where appropriate. Display per-class, language and domain errors with original evidence. Use separate calibration and test sets before displaying calibrated confidence or selecting an abstention threshold; raw softmax is not a reliability guarantee. MVP: Indonesian and one local-language slice, plus a separately authored or consented challenge set for negation, mixed aspects and domain vocabulary. Keep original/translated versions together when splitting. ([NusaX repository](https://github.com/IndoNLP/nusax), [IndoNLU](https://github.com/IndoNLP/indonlu))

**Reuse and optional defence.** Customers could include sentiment vendors, contact centres, localisation teams, agencies and researchers. An optional defence test set examines routine rescue/military words that a classifier misreads. NATO's handbook documents that failure mode; its 2026 language study supports evaluation by language, without validating Indonesian labels.

**Existing alternatives and bounded gap.** Open-source metric scripts and ML evaluation tooling already exist, alongside domestic sentiment research and vendor validation. The proposed wedge is curated Indonesian challenge slices, review evidence and repeatable regression packs. No reviewed public documentation established an identical domestic product; willingness to pay remains untested.

**Evaluation and hardest assumption.** Report macro-F1 by slice, annotator agreement, regression detection and error versus retained coverage after calibration. Test on held-out original examples, domains and time periods. The hardest assumption is obtaining reliable native-speaker/domain annotation economically. This is a stronger specialist tooling direction than a frontline hackathon story; basic reliability checks belong inside NarrativeLens without building this separate product too.

## Prove reuse with two datasets and one workflow

For NarrativeLens, build **import → confirm event/target → inspect source/aspect comparison → review evidence → export brief**. Reuse the same schema and UI for a service disruption and public emergency-information case. Keep record/source identifiers, timestamps, original text, rights metadata and duplicate groups; store target/aspect labels, evidence spans, model version and human corrections as traceable outputs. Change labels and data between templates, rather than writing separate sector applications.

CSV, permitted RSS or owner-authorised exports are sufficient. SQLite, familiar Python tooling and a simple dashboard can support the replay; choose a larger Indonesian model only if it improves the measured task. No universal access to X, Instagram, TikTok or private messaging is assumed. Public visibility does not settle redistribution/training rights. NusaX distinguishes its dataset's CC-BY-SA licence from Apache-2.0 code; inspect obligations for the intended use. ([NusaX licensing](https://github.com/IndoNLP/nusax))

The acceptance test is practical: one held-out incident, source/time counts visible, no inflation from duplicate news, target-aware errors compared with whole-document sentiment, and every brief finding traceable to supporting excerpts. Test slang, negation, mixed aspects, source changes and language/domain drift. Partition by event, time and duplicate group to prevent leakage; measure fresh labels when the source mix changes. Redact unnecessary identifiers and retain only needed evidence. None of this makes the sample representative of Indonesia or supports individual threat scoring.

Before a production investment, ask prospective users to replay one real decision, request competitor demonstrations of matched comparisons and evidence revision, and secure a licensed corpus with independent annotations. These checks answer whether the narrow workflow is valuable and differentiated. They cannot be replaced by finding another foreign defence announcement.

The revised opportunity is a reusable analytical product whose quality is demonstrated in a sensitive deployment context. **NarrativeLens offers the clearest starting point; data access and matching quality should determine whether it proceeds.** A credible defence demonstration strengthens the pitch while leaving the product usable for the next customer and competition.

**Not checked:** authenticated vendor trials, internal Indonesian defence systems, interviews, model/API execution or asset-specific legal review. Equivalent capabilities may already exist privately; no claim of Indonesian firstness or measured product superiority is supported.
